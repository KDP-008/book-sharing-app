Train/onboard engineers
---
“Given the size and maturity of the wider platform team, mentoring and onboarding were critical. I follow what I call the 3 C’s technique:

Context – I start by giving engineers a clear understanding of the application through architecture walkthroughs, data flow diagrams, and how our service fits inside the larger MFE ecosystem. This helps them connect their work to the bigger picture, especially since the platform already had multiple services running for years.

Clarity – I set clear expectations early around coding standards, API design practices, testing requirements, and performance constraints. Since we were dealing with a mix of OpenAPI-generated components and custom modules, clarity helped avoid rework and made contributions consistent.

Confidence – I assign ownership of well-defined modules and pair newer engineers with senior developers initially. This gives them a safe space to learn, ask technical questions, and gradually build confidence. Once they’re comfortable, they take end-to-end responsibility, which boosts productivity and keeps the team unblocked.

“What does technical leadership mean to you?”
---

“For me, technical leadership is about setting the right architectural direction, enabling the team to build effectively, and removing ambiguity. It’s not just making decisions — it’s ensuring the team understands the ‘why,’ feels supported, and can deliver with confidence. It’s also about improving systems sustainably rather than only reacting to short-term pressures.”


“How do you handle disagreements in architecture or design?”
---

“I try to anchor the conversation around clarity — what problem we’re trying to solve, what constraints matter most, and what trade-offs we’re willing to accept. If opinions differ, I bring data where possible — benchmarks, projections, past experience. And if needed, we run small proofs-of-concept to validate assumptions quickly. The goal is alignment, not winning.”


“What’s your approach to designing scalable systems?”
---

“I focus on understanding data flow first — volume, concurrency, latency expectations — because those shape the architecture. Then I design for horizontal scaling, clear separation of concerns, and predictable resource usage. I also look at observability early, since scale without insight leads to fragility. Performance testing and iterative tuning have been key in my recent work.”
